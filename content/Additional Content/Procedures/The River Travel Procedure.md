> _The face of the water, in time, became a wonderful book._
> 
> – Mark Twain, _Life on the Mississippi_ (1883)

The **River Travel Procedure** hangs directly off the [[Downloads#Zines|Worldwide Adventure Generator]] loop and introduces rules that support a dynamic mode of travel, encouraging game masters and players to take to the waterways of their worlds!

While the party is underway, the river confers a number of advantages, including:

- **They cannot become lost.** No navigation check, in any weather, at any hour.
- **They take no fatigue from distance.**
- **Fishing run 1-in-2,** rather than 1-in-5.
- **No wandering encounter check is made.** Encounters occur only through the Complication Table.
- **The boat carries** cargo, casualties, and anything else, within reason, practically for free.

Against that, one thing: it all rides on the boat. Once the party has packed their belongings and their lives into it, a damaged boat is not simply a delay: it could be a disaster. The rations, the map, the powder, and the elixirs, all into the drink with a single mishap, and then—gone.
## Step 1: Class the Water
A single river can have many personalities, each heavily influenced by the land through which it runs. It will rush down from the highlands and linger at its delta; it will crash through steep, rocky canyons and meander across broad, flat plains. To account for this, it is recommended that rivers be classed by *stretches* (which themselves are best managed as 6-mile hexes).

A ***Slow*** stretch of river is broad, deep, and patient, and possesses features like braids, backwaters, and bars. A ***Medium*** stretch of river is a real working river and maintains a quick but controllable pace. A ***Fast*** stretch of river is often characterized by racing white water, and while it covers much ground, the danger can be immense.

| Class  | Current          | Travel Speed | Distance Per Day (Average) | Upstream Penalty                         | Complication    |
| ------ | ---------------- | ------------ | -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| Slow   | 1-2 mph          | 3 mph        | 30 miles, or 5 hexes       | 20%                                      | 1-in-10 per hex |
| Medium | 3-5 mph          | 6 mph        | 60 miles, or 10 hexes      | 80%                                      | 1-in-5 per hex  |
| Fast   | 6 mph and better | 8-10 mph     | 80+ miles, or 13+ hexes    | Not navigable; portage or haul overland. | 1-in-2 per hex  |

>**Travel Speed Reference:** Foot travel is 3 mph over an eight-hour day: 24 miles, or 4 hexes, with 5-6 hexes achievable by forced-marched—which induces fatigue. The waterway's advantage is partly speed and partly the longer day: a current works while the crew rests, and the decreased exertion allows for more traveling hours in the day. 

>**Mapping the River:** Note that there are two obvious methods GMs can use to determine the class of the river: the first is to map it out according to the geography of the surrounding country; the second is to roll each hex, and then record the results into your world canon.
## Step 2: Run the Hexes
At the beginning of the day, roll **Weather (Table A)** once. Then take the hexes one at a time, and for each, check for a complication at the 1-in-X chance dictated the stretch's class.

A hex is two hours of Slow water, a little over an hour of Medium, and fifty minutes of Fast. Fast water is therefore dangerous twice over: more can go wrong in a mile of it, and the miles arrive sooner.

On no result, the hex passes swiftly. GMs are encouraged to give it a line of bank: a burned fishing village; smoke on a distant ridge; a stone that looks as though it were shaped by someone. Roll **Features (Table B)** and **Signs and Omens (Table C)** at whatever interval is preferred—generally once or twice a day, rather than every hex. From midstream the party sees a great deal of country, though very little of it in any detail.
### Generate Complications
The reward of river travel is not without risk. If a complication is encountered on a stretch, roll 1d10 to determine what calamity occurs.

| 1d10 | Complication                | Description                                                            | Outcome                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ---- | --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1    | Snag or sawyer              | A drowned tree or submerged rock lurks in the channel.                 | T5-8 Reflex Check to steer clear; on a failure, roll on the Mishap Table.                                                                                                                     |
| 2    | Bar or shoal                | The boat grinds to a halt on gravel the water gave no sign of.         | T6 Power Check to pole free; on a failure, the party loses 1d2 hours getting it free.                                                                                                            |
| 3    | White water ahead           | The sound and current arrive well before the sight of it.              | T7 Intellect Check to read a line through; failing that, run it blind and roll again at -2, or portage around the stretch; a failed attempt to run it blind requires a roll on the Mishap Table. |
| 4    | The river refuses passage   | Falls, logjams, rockbeds, or something else blocking the way.          | A half-day's portage.                                                                                                                                                                            |
| 5    | Weather turns               | The sky closes and the water rises to meet it.                         | Reroll Table A, skewed toward foul conditions; the party either takes to the shore, or presses on at -2 to all checks.                                                                           |
| 6    | The boat needs repairing    | A seam works loose, a lashing parts, or a pole goes overboard.         | T6 Intellect Check ashore to repair, requires half a day. Until then the boat runs at -3 to all checks; in the event a Complication occurs, roll on the Mishap Table in addition to the result.  |
| 7    | Cargo shifts                | The boat takes a bad roll and the load goes over on itself.            | T5-8 Reflex Check to stabilize; on a failure, 1d5 stowed items go into the water—roll to determine which.                                                                                        |
| 8    | The channel was misread     | A braid, a backwater, or a bend that leads the party astray.           | T5-8 Intellect Check to catch it early; on a failure, the party is forced to backtrack upriver.                                                                                                  |
| 9    | Something at the bank stirs | Movement in the willows, keeping pace, casting shadows, making sounds. | Roll an encounter on Tables D and E, tinted to water and bankside.                                                                                                                               |
| 10   | Another boat                | Someone else is working this river, and they saw you first.            | Roll for reaction.                                                                                                                                                                               |
### Resolve Mishaps
Worse even than complications, mishaps represent an existential threat to the expedition, and can spell the beginning of a long and brutal end.

| 1d10 | Mishap               | Description                                                                            | Outcome                                                                                                                         |
| ---- | -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Total loss           | The boat breaks up and goes down, and the river scatters the pieces irrecoverably.     | Every PC is tossed into the water and keeps only what is worn or in hand.                                                       |
| 2    | Broken keel          | The boat grounds hard and will not float again.                                        | After brought to shore, it requires 1d5 hours to strip its contents; half the cargo is saved. The party is afoot from this hex. |
| 3    | Capsized             | The boat rolls, and everything not tied down goes under with it.                       | Every PC is in the water; T5-8 Power Check to right the boat; up to half the cargo may be recoverable.                          |
| 4    | Swamped              | She fills to the gunwales and wallows.                                                 | T6 Reflex Check for each PC or go over the side; everything stowed is soaked—powder, rations, paper, and all.                   |
| 5    | Holed below the line | Water comes in faster than it can be thrown out.                                       | Up to one hex to reach a landing before sinking. T5 Reflex Check to keep the boat afloat; on a failure, roll again here at -2.  |
| 6    | Sprung seam          | She leaks steadily and someone must bail from here on.                                 | Half speed until repaired, which costs a day ashore and a T6 Intellect Check.                                                   |
| 7    | Steering gone        | The sweep is carried away, or the rudder, one too many paddles, or the last good pole. | The party takes whatever channel the river gives them, and all Complication Checks are at -2 until it is replaced.              |
| 8    | Cargo casualty       | The hull holds, but the load does not.                                                 | 1d5 items go over the side; roll to determine which.                                                                            |
| 9    | Shipped water        | One green wave over the bow, and one thing is ruined.                                  | Roll to determine which.                                                                                                        |
| 10   | Scraped through      | The bottom grates, the hull holds, and the pilot learns something important.           | Nothing is lost, and the party takes +2 on the next Complication.                                                               |
If a PC goes into the water, they must succeed a T5-7 Power Check to reach the boat or the bank. On a failure, they are swept downstream and must try again; on a second failure, they are swept into the next hex and must try once more; on a third failure, they drown.

Additionally, heavyweight armor or a full pack takes a PC under unless shed, requiring one action and a successful T8 Reflex Check.
## Step 3: Landing
Whether putting in to investigate a sighting or make camp for the evening, landing terminates the RTP and returns the party to the ordinary WAG loop in that hex, encounter check and all.
## Notes
- **Portaging** – depending on the nature of the vessel (e.g. the heavy dugouts common to South American tribes or the light cedar canoes of the North American), portaging speeds will vary. A good rule of thumb is **an hour per mile, per trip.** Count how many trips the [[Encumbrance|load demands]], then spend an hour of daylight for each mile of each one.
	- **Fatigue Check**: Each PC should conduct a daily fatigue check; if failed, they suffer fatigue the following day (-1 Reflex or Power).
- **Tinting** – a temperate river is riddled with snags and timber drives; a jungle river is plagued by fever and a closed canopy; an icy river will force an Aspect Save before a swim on any PCs tossed overboard; an underground or enchanted river has Table C carrying more weight than Table B. Doing this on the fly requires that the GM has a solid foundation of River Adventure Stories, the best of which include: 
	- *Life on the Mississippi* (1883) by Mark Twain
	- *Through the Brazilian Wilderness* (1914) by Theodore Roosevelt
	- *Voyages from Montreal* (1801) by Alexander Mackenzie
	- *The Willows* (1907) by Algernon Blackwood
	- *A Week on the Concord and Merrimack Rivers* (1849) by Henry David Thoreau
